# Literature-review readiness for the AE-level analysis

The material in `../../Literature_Review` is sufficient to write a focused analysis and discussion section for the richer AE-level dataset. It already contains the primary SPECTRUM publication, trial-registry context, a defensible source map for the known increase in severe toxicities with panitumumab, and methodological references for semicontinuous and zero-inflated outcomes. The new analysis should use the review as a source map, not cite the review documents themselves.

The primary clinical anchor should remain Vermorken et al. (2013), the randomized SPECTRUM trial report. The appropriate manuscript claim is directional: the positive effect on distinct grade 3+ AE calendar days is consistent with the trial's reported excess of several grade 3/4 toxicities in the panitumumab arm. Published incidence and maximum-grade tables cannot validate a numerical difference in days, because they do not report AE duration or overlap. The data dictionary now supplies the endpoint's operational definitions, including inclusive study-day boundaries and imputation variables.

The review also supports the methodological rationale. Liu et al. supports analysis of zero-inflated nonnegative continuous outcomes, Duan supports retransformation on the original day scale, and the ZIC-BCF and related zero-inflated causal-inference references support flexible two-part causal estimation. The Gamma-hurdle model should be described as a prespecified parametric benchmark, not as an external clinical validation.

Three checks are needed before turning the review into final manuscript prose. First, verify every numerical toxicity claim against the primary paper or its supplement rather than relying on a generated review summary. Second, distinguish the recorded baseline `HPV` category from the trial literature's p16/HPV biomarker analyses; they are biologically related but not automatically interchangeable. Third, revise all wording from "cumulative AE duration" to "distinct calendar days with at least one grade 3+ AE" when describing the new primary or sensitivity endpoint, and explicitly state that concurrent AEs are united rather than double-counted.

With those safeguards, the existing literature review is enough for the analysis portion of the paper. No additional literature is required to justify the endpoint construction itself; its definition follows directly from the supplied AE data dictionary. A broader literature search would only be needed if the manuscript aims to make comparative claims about other trials' AE-duration or overlap-adjusted burden, because the current sources do not provide a like-for-like external endpoint.
